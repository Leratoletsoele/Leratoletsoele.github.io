# GoClean — Agile Team Charter

## 1. Project Vision

Our team developed GoClean as a proposed laundry pickup and delivery service for university students and busy township residents. Our vision is to make laundry more convenient by offering collection, washing, drying, folding and delivery.

The project addresses the time and transport challenges that can make doing laundry inconvenient for our target customers. We propose a digital platform through which customers can access information and manage their laundry orders.

## 2. Project Objectives

Our objectives are to:

- Develop a practical laundry pickup and delivery service concept.
- Identify our target customers and their needs.
- Plan a digital platform with customer registration, login, booking, order confirmation, tracking and order history.
- Apply Agile project-management practices to organise and prioritise our work.
- Divide requirements into smaller tasks and organise them into sprints.
- Document our decisions, progress, challenges and lessons learned.

## 4. Project Scope

Our agreed project concept is a laundry pickup and delivery service that includes collection, washing, drying, folding and delivery.

The proposed digital platform includes:

- Welcome and landing screen
- Registration and login
- Home page
- Order booking and confirmation
- Order tracking and history
- User profile and settings
- Notifications
- Driver assignment
- Payments

Our Trello board organises these features through a product backlog and sprint lists. This structure helps us divide the project into manageable pieces of work.

## 5. Communication and Collaboration

Our team uses formal and informal meetings to discuss the project, share ideas and make decisions. We use Trello as a shared workspace to organise features, assign subtasks and monitor progress.

Our working approach is to communicate respectfully, share project information, keep tasks visible and review outstanding work. We aim to support one another and ensure that our individual contributions align with the project objectives.

## 6. Project Direction and Decision-Making

Our team initially considered developing a digital public healthcare clinic appointment and patient management system. Following feedback about the feasibility and complexity of the original idea, we changed our project direction to GoClean.

The original concept raised concerns about healthcare-related complexity, integrations, funding and the resources available to a student team. We therefore chose a laundry service concept that we could investigate and plan within our project constraints.

This decision helped us establish a clearer scope and identify features that could be organised into user stories, backlog items and sprint tasks.

## 7. Task Organisation and Progress

Our Trello board contains a product backlog and separate sprint lists. These help us organise proposed features and divide them into smaller tasks.

For example, the Login Page card contains five completed subtasks relating to screen design, secure credential storage, the login API, error handling and documentation. The checklist shows 100% completion, and the activity panel records task activity.

This example demonstrates how a feature can be broken down into manageable tasks and tracked using Trello. Completion of this card does not mean that every feature in the overall GoClean project is complete.

## 8. Challenges and Areas for Improvement

Changing our original project idea required us to reconsider our priorities and establish a new project scope. This highlighted the importance of responding to feedback and choosing a project that can be planned realistically with available resources.

We can improve our teamwork by making responsibilities clear, updating Trello consistently, discussing obstacles during meetings and recording decisions. We also need to distinguish between planned features, completed tasks and features that have been tested.

## 9. Personal Reflection

Through this project, I have learned that teamwork requires communication, flexibility and realistic planning. Changing our original idea showed me why it is important to consider feedback and assess whether a project is achievable with the resources available.

Using Trello has helped me understand how a project can be divided into features, subtasks and sprint activities. The Login Page checklist demonstrates how a larger requirement can be broken down into smaller tasks and how progress can be recorded.

I have also learned that clear documentation is important when working in a team. Going forward, I would contribute to clearer responsibility allocation, regular progress updates and better recording of project decisions and testing evidence.
