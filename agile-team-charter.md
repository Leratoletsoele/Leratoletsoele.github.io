# GoClean — Agile Team Charter

## 1. Project Vision

GoClean is a proposed affordable laundry pickup and delivery service aimed at university students and busy township residents. The concept is intended to make laundry more convenient by proposing a service that includes collection, washing, drying, folding and delivery.

The project addresses the time and transport challenges that may make laundry inconvenient for our target customers. We propose a digital platform through which customers could access service information and manage their laundry orders.

GoClean has not yet started collecting, washing or delivering customers' laundry. The project therefore focuses on developing and evaluating the business concept, planning the proposed digital platform and organising the work required for future development. The long-term vision is to investigate whether the service could provide a convenient and feasible solution for the intended customers.

## 2. Project Objectives

The objectives of the GoClean project are to:

- Develop a practical and feasible laundry pickup and delivery service concept.
- Identify the intended target customers and investigate their potential needs.
- Plan a digital platform that includes registration, login, order booking, order confirmation, order tracking and order history.
- Apply Agile principles to organise requirements, prioritise features and structure project work.
- Break larger requirements into smaller tasks that can be managed and tracked.
- Organise proposed features into sprint lists to support incremental planning.
- Use Trello to maintain visibility of backlog items and recorded task progress.
- Document project decisions, challenges, evidence and lessons learned.
- Identify future steps, including customer-demand validation and pilot testing, before treating the proposed service as operational.

These objectives provide direction for the project and help connect the business concept to its planned application features and Agile artefacts.

## 4. Project Scope

The GoClean project focuses on planning a proposed laundry pickup and delivery service and its supporting digital platform.

The proposed service concept includes:

- Collection of customers' laundry.
- Washing and drying.
- Folding clean laundry.
- Delivery of completed laundry to customers.

The proposed digital platform includes the following features:

- Welcome and landing screen.
- Customer registration and login.
- Home page.
- Laundry order booking and confirmation.
- Order tracking and order history.
- User profile and settings.
- Notifications.
- Driver assignment.
- Payment functionality.

These features are organised on our Trello board through product backlog items and sprint lists. This approach helps the team break down the proposed solution into smaller areas of work.

The scope describes the intended service and application features; it does not mean that all features have been implemented, tested or deployed. Operating the laundry service, processing real customer orders and validating demand remain future steps.

## 5. Communication and Collaboration

Our team uses formal and informal discussions to exchange ideas, consider feedback and make project decisions. Trello provides a shared workspace for organising features, recording subtasks and viewing progress where checklist and activity information is available.

Effective collaboration requires team members to communicate respectfully, share relevant project information and raise obstacles that may affect progress. It also requires a shared understanding of priorities and the status of outstanding work.

One area for improvement is to maintain more consistent records of responsibilities, decisions and progress. Clearer records would make it easier to identify who is responsible for particular tasks, understand what has been completed and follow up on outstanding work.

## 6. Project Direction and Decision-Making

Our team initially considered developing a digital public healthcare clinic appointment and patient management system. Following feedback about the feasibility and complexity of the original concept, we changed our direction to GoClean.

The initial idea raised concerns about the complexity of healthcare-related requirements, potential integrations and the resources available to a student team. The team therefore selected a laundry pickup and delivery concept that could be explored and planned within the project's constraints.

This change demonstrates the importance of considering feedback, reassessing assumptions and adapting the project scope when an initial idea may be difficult to deliver. It also gave the team an opportunity to identify a more focused set of proposed customer needs and application features.

The experience reinforced the value of evaluating feasibility before committing to a project direction. In future projects, we would benefit from recording the alternatives considered, the reasons for a decision and the actions that follow from it.

## 7. Task Organisation and Progress

Our Trello board contains a product backlog and separate sprint lists. These help organise proposed features and divide requirements into smaller tasks.

One example is the Login Page card. Its checklist contains five completed subtasks relating to screen design, secure credential storage, the login API, error handling and documentation. The card displays 100% checklist completion, and the activity panel provides a record of task activity.

This evidence demonstrates how a feature can be broken down into manageable subtasks and how checklist progress can be recorded. However, checklist completion alone does not independently establish that every part of the feature has been integrated, tested successfully or deployed.

It is also important to distinguish the progress recorded on this card from the status of the overall project. The Login Page checklist is evidence of progress on that particular item; it does not demonstrate that every proposed GoClean feature or the laundry service itself is complete.

In future, task tracking could be strengthened by recording task ownership, linking completed work to relevant deliverables and adding testing evidence where available.

## 8. Challenges and Areas for Improvement

One significant challenge was changing the original project idea after receiving feedback about its feasibility and complexity. This required the team to reconsider its direction and establish a new project scope.

The experience showed that project planning must remain flexible and that feedback should be used to reassess whether an idea is achievable with the resources available.

Another area for improvement is project coordination and documentation. More consistent records of task ownership, decisions, obstacles and testing outcomes would make progress easier to review. The team should also distinguish clearly between proposed features, tasks with recorded checklist progress and functionality that has been verified through testing.

Future improvements could include agreeing on responsibilities earlier, reviewing outstanding work regularly, recording important decisions and gathering customer feedback before making claims about demand for the proposed service.

## 9. Personal Reflection

Through the GoClean project, I have developed a better understanding of the relationship between Agile planning, teamwork and project feasibility. I learned that a project idea must be evaluated in terms of customer needs, complexity and available resources rather than being pursued simply because it was the original plan.

The change from a healthcare management concept to a proposed laundry service demonstrated the importance of responding to feedback and adapting the project scope. It also showed me that a clearer project direction makes it easier to identify relevant features and organise them into manageable pieces of work.

Working with the project's Agile artefacts has helped me understand how user stories describe customer needs, how the product backlog organises proposed requirements and how sprint lists group work into stages. The Login Page checklist provides a practical example of task breakdown and recorded progress.

I have also recognised that good documentation must be supported by reliable evidence. A task marked complete is useful evidence of recorded progress, but additional testing or deliverable evidence may be needed to confirm that the intended functionality works as expected.

If I were contributing to a similar project in the future, I would encourage clearer responsibility allocation, more consistent progress records and earlier discussion of obstacles. I would also make sure that project decisions and claims about completed work were supported by evidence. These improvements would make collaboration more transparent and help the team evaluate progress more accurately.

Overall, the project has taught me that Agile involves more than dividing work into tasks. It also involves adapting to feedback, communicating effectively, evaluating evidence and continuously improving the way a team plans and delivers its work.
