
# GoClean — Agile Logbook

## 1. Introduction

An Agile logbook records important project activities, decisions, progress and lessons learned throughout the development process. It provides a chronological account of how a team organises its work, responds to feedback and applies Agile principles.

The GoClean project focused on developing a laundry pickup and delivery application designed to provide convenient and affordable laundry services to university students and busy residents. The project journey included selecting a suitable concept, responding to lecturer feedback, identifying customer needs, organising features and tasks, developing the application, testing the completed project and preparing the supporting portfolio documentation.

This logbook documents the key stages of the GoClean project and reflects on my learning in feasibility, teamwork, prioritisation, backlog management, sprint organisation and project documentation.

## 3. Meetings and Team Communication

Communication played an important role in organising the GoClean project. Team collaboration involved considering the project's direction, discussing its features and arranging tasks around the application's main customer journey.

Trello provided a shared workspace where the team could organise backlog items, group related work into sprint lists, assign team members to task cards and record checklist progress. This visual approach helped make project activities easier to review and supported coordination among team members.

The project also taught me the value of keeping project information organised. Clear records help team members understand decisions, responsibilities and the progress associated with different activities.

Important communication and documentation practices include:

- Recording meeting dates and the team members who attended.
- Documenting the main topics discussed.
- Keeping a record of significant decisions.
- Assigning responsibilities to individual tasks.
- Sharing progress updates with team members.
- Recording relevant changes and their reasons.
- Keeping task information organised in Trello.

These practices support accountability, collaboration and a shared understanding of the project's objectives.

## 4. Challenges and Lessons Learned

One of the key challenges during the GoClean project was selecting a concept with a suitable scope. The original idea focused on a digital healthcare clinic appointment and patient management system. After lecturer feedback, the team changed direction and developed the laundry pickup and delivery concept.

This experience taught me that feedback is an important part of Agile development. Being willing to reconsider an idea helped the team establish a clearer project direction and focus on a service with an identifiable target audience.

Another important lesson was the value of breaking a project into manageable features and tasks. The product backlog organised the application's functionality, while sprint groupings brought related features together. Trello task cards and checklists provided a practical way to record activities and their progress.

The project also strengthened my understanding of prioritisation. Features such as customer access and laundry booking support the central customer journey, while features such as notifications, payment functionality and profile management contribute to the wider service experience.

Testing the completed GoClean project helped me appreciate the relationship between planned requirements and the finished application. I also learned that project evidence, including interface screenshots and recorded task progress, helps communicate the work carried out by the team.

Finally, preparing the portfolio reinforced the importance of documentation. A well-organised project record makes it easier to explain the decisions, tasks, evidence and learning associated with a software development project.

## 5. Personal Reflection

Preparing this logbook helped me understand that Agile is more than dividing a project into sprints. It involves responding to feedback, organising requirements, prioritising work, communicating with team members and reflecting on the development process.

The decision to move from the healthcare concept to GoClean taught me that adaptability is an important part of teamwork. I learned to consider feedback carefully and recognise how a change in project direction can help establish a clearer focus.

Working with the product backlog and sprint groupings improved my understanding of how a broad idea can be divided into individual features and manageable tasks. Trello helped organise the team's work and provided a shared space for viewing task assignments and recorded checklist progress.

Completing and testing GoClean allowed me to connect the planning activities with the finished application. I gained a better understanding of how user requirements, interface design, task organisation and testing contribute to the overall development process.

I also learned the importance of supporting project documentation with evidence. Interface screenshots show the application's design, while Trello cards and checklists demonstrate how tasks were organised and progress was recorded.

Overall, this project strengthened my understanding of Agile planning, feasibility, prioritisation, teamwork and project documentation. It also improved my ability to reflect on decisions and explain how individual activities contributed to the completed project. These skills will be valuable when participating in future software development projects.
