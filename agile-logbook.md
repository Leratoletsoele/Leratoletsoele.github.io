
# GoClean — Agile Logbook

## 1. Introduction

An Agile logbook documents important project activities, decisions, progress, challenges and lessons learned throughout a project's development. It provides a record of how the team organised its work, responded to feedback and reflected on its approach.

For GoClean, this logbook documents the journey from the initial project concept to the planning and documentation of a proposed laundry pickup and delivery service. It highlights the importance of feasibility, customer needs, teamwork, backlog management and sprint planning.

The logbook also reflects on my personal learning and identifies improvements that could strengthen the team's Agile practices in future projects.

## 3. Meetings and Team Communication

Communication is an important part of Agile project management because team members need to share ideas, discuss requirements, make decisions, allocate responsibilities and address challenges.

During the GoClean project, the team had to consider the project's direction, scope, proposed features and organisation of tasks. Trello provided a shared space for arranging features, grouping work into sprints and recording checklist progress.

Consistent communication and documentation are areas that could be strengthened. A more detailed record of team meetings, attendance and decisions would make it easier to understand how the project developed and how responsibilities were managed.

For future projects, the team should record:

- The date of each meeting and the team members who attended.
- The main topics discussed.
- Decisions made and the reasons behind them.
- Tasks assigned and the responsible team members.
- Challenges identified and actions agreed upon.
- Progress updates and outstanding tasks.

Maintaining these records would improve accountability, support collaboration and provide stronger evidence of the team's Agile process.

## 4. Challenges and Lessons Learned

One of the main challenges was selecting a project concept that could be managed within the team's available time, skills and resources. The change from the healthcare system idea to GoClean highlighted the importance of evaluating feasibility before committing to a project's full scope.

Another lesson was the value of breaking a large project into manageable features and tasks. The product backlog helped organise the proposed functionality, while sprint planning provided a structure for grouping related work into stages.

The project also strengthened my understanding of prioritisation. Features that support the main customer journey, such as logging in and booking a laundry service, need to be considered alongside supporting features such as notifications, payment functionality and profile management.

I also learned the importance of distinguishing between planned work, recorded checklist progress and verified functionality. Keeping the Trello board updated is useful, but project reporting is stronger when task records are supported by relevant screenshots, demonstrations or test results.

Finally, the project highlighted the need for consistent documentation. Recording decisions, task owners, changes and progress would help the team identify outstanding work and understand why particular decisions were made.

## 5. Personal Reflection

Preparing this logbook has helped me understand that Agile is more than dividing a project into sprints. It involves responding to feedback, adapting to changing requirements, prioritising tasks, communicating with team members and reflecting on the work completed.

The decision to move from the healthcare concept to GoClean showed me that a project may need to change direction when its original scope presents practical challenges. I learned that accepting feedback and reconsidering an idea can help a team develop a more manageable project plan.

Working with the product backlog and sprint groupings also helped me understand how a broad idea can be organised into smaller features and tasks. Trello made the proposed work more visible and provided a way to record task-level progress.

One area I would improve in future projects is the consistency of our project records. I would encourage the team to document meeting outcomes, assign clear task owners, update the board regularly and link each backlog item to its tasks and acceptance criteria. I would also ensure that testing outcomes are recorded when testing takes place.

Overall, this activity improved my understanding of Agile planning, feasibility, prioritisation and evidence-based reflection. I learned that effective project management requires both a clear plan and reliable records of decisions and progress. These lessons will help me approach future group projects in a more organised, collaborative and reflective way.
