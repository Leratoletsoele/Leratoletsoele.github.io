

# GoClean — Agile Logbook

## 1. Introduction

An Agile logbook records important project activities, decisions, progress, challenges and reflections throughout a project. It helps team members review what happened, identify lessons learned and consider what needs to happen next.

For GoClean, this logbook records the project's development, from the initial idea to the planning and documentation of the proposed laundry pickup and delivery service. It includes reflections on how feedback, teamwork, backlog organisation and sprint planning contributed to the project.

The logbook distinguishes between activities supported by available project evidence and activities recorded as part of the overall project timeline. This helps ensure that the portfolio represents the project accurately.

## 3. Meetings and Team Communication

Communication is important in Agile project work because team members need to discuss requirements, make decisions, organise tasks and identify problems that may affect progress.

During the development of the GoClean concept, the project required consideration of its direction, scope, proposed features and task organisation. The Trello board provides a shared space for organising work and reviewing recorded task progress.

A complete record of meeting dates, attendance and detailed minutes was not available when this logbook was prepared. Therefore, specific meeting dates and daily stand-ups are not presented as confirmed events.

For future project work, the team should record:

- The date of each meeting.
- The team members who participated.
- The topics discussed.
- Decisions made and the reasons for them.
- Tasks assigned and the responsible team members.
- Challenges identified and actions requiring follow-up.

Keeping these records would make progress easier to track and provide stronger evidence of communication, decision-making and collaboration.

## 4. Challenges and Lessons Learned

One challenge was selecting a project idea that could be developed within the team's available time and resources. The change from the healthcare concept to GoClean demonstrates the importance of reviewing project scope and responding to feedback.

Another lesson was the importance of breaking work into manageable features and tasks. The Trello board helps organise proposed features into sprint lists, while individual card checklists provide a way to record task-level progress.

I also learned that planned work, checklist completion and verified functionality are different things. A task marked as complete on Trello is useful evidence of recorded progress, but it does not automatically demonstrate that a software feature works correctly.

A further area for improvement is maintaining consistent records of meetings, decisions, task responsibilities and changes to sprint plans. Better documentation would help the team understand why decisions were made and identify outstanding work more easily.

The project also highlighted the importance of feasibility. A project idea may be valuable, but the team must consider whether it can realistically be planned and developed within the available time, resources and skills.

## 5. Personal Reflection

Preparing this logbook has helped me understand that Agile is not only about dividing development into sprints. It also involves responding to feedback, organising work, communicating decisions and reflecting on progress.

The GoClean project pivot showed me that a team may need to reconsider its original idea when feedback or practical constraints suggest that a different direction may be more feasible. This reinforced the importance of reviewing scope before committing to a development plan.

The product backlog and sprint groupings helped me understand how a broad project idea can be divided into smaller features and tasks. Trello also provides a way to organise responsibilities and record checklist progress. However, I learned that a project board should be supported by accurate records and appropriate evidence when reporting what has actually been achieved.

If I were improving our process, I would encourage the team to record meeting dates and outcomes more consistently, assign clear task owners and update Trello as work progresses. I would also encourage the team to connect backlog items to their corresponding tasks and acceptance criteria, then record test outcomes when testing has taken place.

Overall, this activity improved my understanding of Agile planning, project adaptability, task organisation and evidence-based reflection. It also taught me that a good logbook should communicate the project journey honestly, including challenges, decisions, areas for improvement and the limits of the available evidence.
