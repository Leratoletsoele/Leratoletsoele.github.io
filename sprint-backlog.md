# GoClean — Sprint Backlog

## 1. Introduction

The sprint backlog is a collection of features and tasks selected for a sprint. It helps the team organise the work that needs to be completed during a development period and monitor progress towards the sprint goal.

For GoClean, our Trello board separates planned features into Sprint 1, Sprint 2 and Sprint 3. These lists organise the proposed customer journey, from accessing the platform to placing and tracking laundry orders and managing customer accounts.

## 2. Sprint Goals and Planned Work

### Sprint 1: Customer Access and Landing Page

**Sprint goal:** Organise the main entry point to GoClean and the features customers need to access the proposed platform.

| Feature | Planned tasks |
|---|---|
| Welcome/Landing Screen | Design the welcome screen and introduce the GoClean service. |
| Home Page | Organise the main navigation and display key service information. |
| Registration Page | Plan the registration form and the information needed to create an account. |
| Login Page | Design the login screen, plan credential storage, build the login API and test error handling. |

The Login Page card on our Trello board contains five subtasks and displays a checklist completion rate of 100%. This provides evidence of progress on that specific card, rather than proof that the entire application is complete.

### Sprint 2: Laundry Orders and Tracking

**Sprint goal:** Organise the features required for customers to request laundry collection and follow the progress of their orders.

| Feature | Planned tasks |
|---|---|
| Order Page | Identify the details customers need to provide when booking laundry collection. |
| Order Confirmation | Plan how customers will receive confirmation after submitting an order. |
| Order Tracking | Identify the order progress information that should be displayed to customers. |
| Order History | Plan how customers can review their previous orders. |

These features support the main GoClean service journey. The detailed tasks should be reviewed and updated by the team in Trello as development progresses.

### Sprint 3: Account Management and Supporting Features

**Sprint goal:** Plan additional features that support customer account management and the proposed pickup and delivery process.

| Feature | Planned tasks |
|---|---|
| User Profile/Settings | Identify the customer information and settings that should be available. |
| Notifications | Identify important order updates that customers may need to receive. |
| Driver Assignment | Plan how deliveries could be assigned and organised. |
| Payments | Identify the payment requirements for the proposed service. |

These items extend the customer journey and support the operational needs of the proposed laundry service. Their inclusion in a sprint list indicates planned work, not necessarily completed implementation.

## 3. Task Organisation and Progress Tracking

Our team uses Trello to organise features and tasks. The separate sprint lists help make the planned work visible and allow team members to review what needs attention.

Breaking features into smaller tasks makes work easier to understand, assign and monitor. Checklists can also show progress on individual cards. For example, the Login Page card has five subtasks marked as complete.

To improve progress tracking, the team should keep each card updated, identify unfinished tasks, communicate difficulties and distinguish completed work from work that is still planned.

## 4. Challenges and Areas for Improvement

One area for improvement is making sure that tasks have clear owners and realistic completion targets. The team should also review dependencies between features. For example, the order booking process may depend on customer account access and the information required to create an order.

Regular updates would help the team identify delays earlier and adjust plans when necessary. Any changes to the sprint backlog should be discussed and recorded so that team members share an understanding of the work.

## 5. Personal Reflection

Working with a sprint backlog has helped me understand how Agile development divides a larger project into smaller, more manageable stages. For GoClean, the sprint lists group related features together, beginning with customer access, moving to ordering and tracking, and then covering additional account and service features.

I have also learned that having a feature listed in a sprint does not mean that it has been completed. Progress needs to be supported by updated tasks and evidence from the team's project board. The Login Page checklist is an example of evidence that can be used to show progress on an individual feature.

In future sprint planning, I would encourage the team to assign clear responsibilities, agree on achievable targets and update Trello consistently. This would make it easier to monitor progress and reflect accurately on the work completed.
